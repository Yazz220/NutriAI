# Nosh pricing and packaging market research

Research date: 2026-08-31

This benchmark uses first-party pricing pages, help centers, Apple developer documentation, and US App Store listings. Dollar prices below are US prices unless a source says otherwise. Mobile and storefront prices can differ because vendors localize pricing and taxes. A blank or qualified trial entry means the vendor does not publish a standing trial in the cited source, not that it has never run a promotion.

## The market in one page

Two price bands matter for Nosh:

- Established recipe and cooking subscriptions cluster around $2.99 to $6.99 per month and $24.99 to $59.99 per year. ReciMe and CookBook currently publish $39.99 US annual plans, while Samsung Food+ reaches $59.99. These products usually leave the organizing and cooking loop usable for free, then meter recipe imports, scans, AI changes, nutrition, sync, or advanced guidance.
- General consumer AI subscriptions start lower than they used to. ChatGPT Go is $8 per month, while ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, and Perplexity Pro sit at $20 per month. Their highest-use plans run from $100 to $200 per month. These products do not promise a simple number of unlimited requests. They sell a useful plan with variable usage allowances, then increasingly offer optional pay-as-you-go credits after the included allowance.

The strongest pattern is subscription plus included usage, not a credits-only product. Credits appear as an overflow mechanism for expensive, variable work. The ordinary chat or core product remains easy to understand.

### What these AI products teach

1. A subscription buys a level of access and a better experience. It does not buy a raw token balance.
2. Vendors reserve credits for costly work with variable compute, such as autonomous research, coding, file creation, or other multi-step agent tasks.
3. The user spends included usage before paid credits. Auto-refill is optional and has a user-set cap.
4. More tiers appear only when a distinct heavy-use segment exists. ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity can support $100 to $200 plans because their professional power users have radically different usage and willingness to pay from ordinary consumers.
5. Exact request limits change. The product shows the live allowance and reset state rather than relying on a fixed promise in App Store copy.

## Broader consumer subscription benchmarks

### Headspace

Headspace is a useful benchmark for a specialist app that now includes an AI companion inside a broader subscription. Its US content plan is $12.99/month with a 7-day trial or $69.99/year with a 14-day trial. The annual price is 55.1% below twelve monthly payments. The subscription unlocks its content library and Ebb, its AI companion, instead of charging per AI conversation. [Headspace plan prices and trials](https://www.headspace.com/mental-health), [Headspace purchase help](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758647-How-do-I-purchase-a-Headspace-subscription), [Ebb packaging](https://www.headspace.com/headspace-subscription/ebb).

This is a strong packaging lesson for Nosh. AI can make the overall product more personal without becoming a separate currency every time the user asks for help.

### Duolingo

Duolingo keeps every course available for free. Super removes ads and usage friction such as limited energy, while Max sits above Super and adds generative AI features such as Video Call, Roleplay, and personalized explanations. The current US App Store page advertises a 14-day Super trial. It lists several Super purchase prices between $9.99 and $95.99 but does not label their duration consistently, so this report does not assign those SKUs to monthly or annual plans. [Current US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/duolingo-language-lessons/id570060128), [free-course and paid-tier structure](https://blog.duolingo.com/does-duolingo-work/), [Max feature positioning](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max/).

Duolingo's important choice is what it does not gate. The complete learning path remains free. Paid plans remove friction and add richer practice, and the highest tier carries the expensive AI experience.

### What the recipe category teaches

- A useful free cookbook is normal. Pestle uses a lifetime collection cap, ReciMe uses a weekly import meter, and Samsung uses small lifetime or storage limits on expensive features.
- Import or capture is the cleanest metered event because users understand that it creates a durable asset. Chat messages, searches, edits, and substitutions are harder to price transparently and are central to habit formation.
- The paid recipe-app ceiling is currently about $59.99/year before Nosh's stronger generated-page and persistent-chef value is considered.
- Annual discounts for Pestle, Samsung Food+, and SideChef range from about 16.5% to 30.3%. CookBook is an aggressive outlier at 52.3%. A discount around 25% is normal and does not devalue the monthly plan.
- A one-time purchase works for Paprika and remains an option for Pestle because much of their value can run locally. It is a poor fit for a product that repeatedly pays for model inference, image generation, transcription, storage, and cloud orchestration.

## App Store rules that affect the model

This is product guidance based on Apple's current public rules, not legal advice.

### Digital access must use In-App Purchase

Nosh unlocks digital functionality inside the app, so the straightforward launch path is Apple's In-App Purchase system. Guideline 3.1.1 says apps must use IAP to unlock subscriptions, premium functionality, content, or a full version. It also says credits bought through IAP may not expire. [App Review Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

Apple defines four relevant product types. A consumable depletes and can be bought again. A non-consumable is bought once and does not expire. An auto-renewable subscription gives dynamic content or services for a period and renews until canceled. A non-renewing subscription must be bought again manually. [Apple's IAP type reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/configure-in-app-purchase-settings/overview-for-configuring-in-app-purchases).

If Nosh later sells extra page-generation packs, those packs fit Apple's consumable type. They must be delivered reliably and purchased credits cannot expire. A lifetime Pro unlock would be a non-consumable, but that would not solve the cost of recurring AI work.

### A Nosh subscription is permissible

Apple permits subscriptions in every category when they deliver ongoing value, last at least seven days, and work on all of the user's supported devices. The guidelines explicitly allow subscriptions to include consumable credits and to coexist with a la carte purchases. Nosh's cloud cookbook, repeated AI capture, generated pages, persistent chef, sync, and ongoing service fit the ongoing-value requirement. [App Review Guidelines 3.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

For a simple launch, Apple recommends one subscription group. Monthly and annual versions with the same access can sit at the same level in that group. A customer can hold only one subscription in a group, which prevents double billing and makes monthly-to-annual switching cleaner. [Subscription group setup](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/offer-auto-renewable-subscriptions/).

### Trial and paywall presentation

Apple allows 3-day, 1-week, 2-week, 1-month, 2-month, 3-month, 6-month, or 1-year free introductory periods for monthly and annual subscriptions. Each customer can redeem only one introductory offer per subscription group. [Introductory offer rules](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/set-up-introductory-offers-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions).

The purchase screen must show the subscription name, duration, included service, full localized renewal price, and a way for existing customers to sign in or restore purchases. Terms of Use and Privacy Policy links must appear in the app and App Store metadata. For annual billing, the total annual charge must be more prominent than a monthly-equivalent calculation. A trial must state its duration and the price charged afterward. [Apple subscription presentation requirements](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/).

Do not hard-code a US dollar price into the paywall. Read the localized display price from StoreKit. Apple offers up to 800 price points by country or region and supports independent storefront pricing. [IAP pricing and availability](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/pricing-and-availability/in-app-purchase-and-subscriptions-pricing-and-availability).

### Apple proceeds

Standard subscription proceeds are 70% during a subscriber's first paid year and 85% afterward, minus taxes. Developers accepted into the App Store Small Business Program receive 85% from day one while eligible. The program is available to new developers and developers with up to $1 million in prior-year proceeds across associated accounts. [Subscription proceeds](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/offer-auto-renewable-subscriptions/), [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/).

For launch economics, model both cases. If Nosh qualifies for Small Business, a $59.99 annual US subscription yields at most about $50.99 before taxes and any refunds. Without the program, first-year proceeds are at most about $41.99 before taxes.

## Evidence-backed implications for Nosh

These are market inferences. Final price and included generation allowance still need Nosh's measured model, image-generation, storage, and support costs.

1. **Use a subscription as the main product.** Nosh provides ongoing cloud and AI value. A one-time unlock would force future generation costs onto a fixed historical payment.
2. **Launch with one paid tier and two billing periods.** One tier keeps the promise legible and matches Pestle, ReciMe, CookBook, Samsung Food+, SideChef, and Headspace. Add a heavy-use tier only after usage data reveals a real segment, not merely because general AI companies have one.
3. **Make Free genuinely useful.** Users should be able to create a small cookbook, read their pages, and experience Nosh. The nearest competitors let users keep 15 recipes, import 5 per week, or use broad organization for free.
4. **Meter durable, expensive outputs.** Complete generated recipe pages, page regeneration, image or video extraction, and transcription have visible value and variable cost. Basic questions about an open recipe, substitutions, browsing saved books, manual edits, and reading should not feel like a taxi meter.
5. **Include usage before selling add-ons.** A paid subscription should include enough generation usage to deliver the complete product promise. The exact amount and reset period should come from Nosh's measured unit economics and usage distribution. If a small group later exceeds that allowance, non-expiring consumable page packs are a cleaner extension than making every action credit-based. This matches the current ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity pattern of plan allowance first and paid overflow second.
6. **Do not lead with the word credits.** Sell outcomes such as recipe pages or extra captures. Internal credits can price different workloads, but users should see a predictable unit whenever possible.
7. **Anchor within the gap between recipe utilities and general AI.** The evidence gives Nosh a plausible launch range above $6.99/month recipe tools but below the $20/month general AI tier, provided the plan includes enough generated pages to feel complete. Annual pricing around the upper recipe band can still look strong if the paywall explains the saved cookbook, designed pages, and persistent chef rather than presenting Nosh as another recipe importer.
8. **Prefer a product-led free allowance over a card-required trial as the only test.** Pestle, ReciMe, and Samsung let people retain useful free accounts after the trial. Nosh needs repeat usage to prove that a saved cookbook and contextual chef become more valuable over time. A trial can still accelerate conversion, but it should sit on top of the free product.

## Pricing facts to carry into the final decision

- Closest monthly anchors: Pestle $2.99, SideChef $4.99, Samsung Food+ $6.99, Headspace $12.99, ChatGPT Go $8, and mainstream AI Pro plans $20.
- Closest annual anchors: Pestle $24.99, ReciMe $39.99, CookBook $39.99, SideChef $49.99, Samsung Food+ $59.99, Headspace $69.99, and Claude or Perplexity Pro $200.
- Typical annual discount among Pestle, Samsung Food+, SideChef, Claude Pro, and Perplexity Pro is about 16.5% to 30.3%. CookBook and Headspace are aggressive outliers at roughly 52.3% and 55.1%.
- The strongest recipe competitors meter imports or advanced AI, not recipe viewing. The strongest AI competitors reserve credit mechanics for costly overflow, not the entire relationship.

Launch one paid tier, **Nosh Plus**, with monthly and annual billing in one App Store subscription group.

The paid allowance should count a successful new rendered page, a successful saved recipe revision, or a successful visual regeneration. It should not count a tap, rejected source, timeout, provider failure, publication retry, unchanged page copy, ordinary chat, search, timer, walkthrough, or session-only substitution or scaling.

This is intentionally one feature tier. Monthly and annual are billing choices, not separate products. Nosh does not yet have evidence for a second power-user tier, and a credits-only storefront would make ordinary cookbook building feel transactional.

### Why this price and allowance are defensible

Nosh currently requests one 2K Qwen Image 3 Pro image for every finished cookbook page. A production aggregate checked on the research date contained 11 successful image requests, each reporting **$0.075**, for **$0.825 total**. The 12 retained page PNGs averaged **3.24 MB**. Chat and extraction are cheaper, but the app does not yet persist their provider cost well enough to calculate a trustworthy blended cost.

At the observed image price, the visible variable-cost floor is:

- Five free pages: **$0.375** in image generation, plus extraction, chat, storage, egress, failures, and support.
- Twenty paid pages in a month: **$1.50** in image generation, plus the same additional costs.
- A fully used annual plan at 20 pages in every month: **$18.00** in image generation.

If Nosh is accepted into Apple's Small Business Program, $9.99 yields at most about $8.49 after Apple's 15% commission, before taxes and refunds. RevenueCat is free through $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue and then charges 1%, so the monthly plan would retain about **$6.89 after Apple, RevenueCat, and all 20 observed-price page images**, before text-model usage, fixed infrastructure, refunds, and support. The $79.99 annual plan would retain about **$49.19** after Apple's 15%, RevenueCat's 1%, and 240 observed-price page images. Model the standard Apple rate as a downside case until Small Business enrollment is confirmed.

Those margins are a launch hypothesis, not proof. The production sample has only three authenticated users and 14 generation requests. Three of those request records failed. That is too little data for a stable failure rate, but enough to require strict no-charge-on-failure accounting before payments launch.

### Why not pure credits

A page pack maps neatly to image cost, but it does not capture the ongoing value of cloud cookbooks, contextual help, sync, maintenance, and future improvements. It also encourages users to avoid the product's best actions. A subscription creates the right relationship; the included page allowance protects the variable-cost side.

Do not add a consumable pack in version 1.0. Add one only if real subscribers regularly reach the allowance and then wait instead of returning or upgrading. A sensible first test would be **10 additional page creations for $2.99**. Apple-purchased units must not expire, so purchased top-ups need a separate non-expiring balance and must never be mixed with the resetting subscription service allowance.

### Required monetization foundation

The current app has no active IAP SDK, paywall, subscription entitlement, restore flow, receipt or webhook handling, or server-enforced quota. The historical internal credit ledger is explicitly dormant and is not suitable as purchase infrastructure.

Before charging users:

1. Add RevenueCat's Expo-compatible SDK and one `nosh_plus` entitlement, backed by monthly and annual App Store products. RevenueCat is currently free up to $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue and charges 1% above that threshold. [RevenueCat pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing), [Expo integration](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/getting-started/installation/expo).
2. Sync RevenueCat webhooks idempotently into Supabase. Treat the server as authoritative for paid AI work; a client paywall is not enforcement.
3. Add an atomic usage ledger that reserves an included page before costly work, settles only after a usable page succeeds, and compensates exactly once after failure. Record operation type, model, provider cost, source request, created page/version, entitlement period, and refund or compensation events.
4. Add plan status, live page allowance and reset date, Restore Purchases, and Manage Subscription to Settings. Existing pages must remain readable after cancellation.
5. Add Terms of Use, purchase-related privacy disclosures, a private billing-support channel, StoreKit sandbox tests, and subscription-aware account-deletion copy before App Review.
6. Enroll in Apple's Small Business Program if eligible, complete the Paid Apps Agreement, tax, and banking setup, and submit the first subscription products with the launch build.

Keep price, allowance, and paywall placement remotely configurable. After launch, review free-to-paid conversion, pages per activated user, paid page use at the 50th and 90th percentiles, percentage hitting the allowance, blended provider cost per successful page, failure compensation, annual-plan share, refund rate, and contribution margin before changing the package.
